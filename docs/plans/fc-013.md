## Design decisions

### DD-1 — Placement: scanner-side gate (both legs), not selection-side, not both

**Chosen:** the gate lives inside `OptionsScanner`. Rev 2.2 refines the call leg's
*granularity* — per-candidate span filtering in the criteria chain — but the
*placement* decision (scan stage, not selection, not both) is unchanged, and its
rationale carries over intact.

**Rejected — selection-side drop (`select_batch`, new `DROP_EARNINGS_BLACKOUT`):**

- `/run` has no earnings data and no natural place to get it: either
  `ExecutionEngine` grows its own Finnhub surface (a second copy of the outage
  problem, in the process that places orders), or the scan stamps proximity onto
  each opportunity and selection reads it — which is scan-time evaluation wearing a
  selection costume, plus blob plumbing.
- `DROP_REASONS` is FC-038's model for **batch-resource contention** — shares,
  buying power, duplicate-underlying — evaluated against *this batch*. Earnings is
  a policy fact known before ranking. Wedging it into the drop model misfiles it.
- Selection-side wastes the scan: chains fetched, opportunities built and ranked
  for candidates that cannot trade, plus (call leg) a BigQuery cost-basis
  cross-check spent on them.
- Observability is not better there: the call leg's drop would reach decision
  records via `/run`, but the scan-side gate reaches them too (§DD-4), and the put
  leg has no decision-record machinery on either side.

**Rejected — gate at both:** no incremental protection. Both predicates are
date-granular; between `/scan` and `/run` sit ≤30 minutes
(`opportunity_max_age_minutes`), inside market hours, never crossing midnight —
neither predicate can change value in that window. Cost: a second Finnhub
dependency surface and a second failure-semantics decision, for nothing.

**Finnhub volume / latency, derived against the real topology (rev 2.1 — rev 2's
numbers assumed ~13 scans/day and warm instances; production runs 6 scans/day,
hourly :00 from 10:00–15:00 ET, on min-instances=0, so instances are typically
reaped between hour-blocks and a module cache alone is cold nearly every scan):**

- The calendar is consulted once per put-side *suitable* stock (≤14) and once per
  call-side held symbol (≤7, overlapping) — ≤14 unique symbols per scan. The call
  leg's per-candidate span test reuses the **same single per-symbol date fetch**;
  candidate count adds zero Finnhub calls.
- **Without durable storage:** ~6 cold scans × 14 = **~84 Finnhub calls/day worst
  case**, each scan paying +2–6s of sequential fetches on the ~18.5s baseline —
  every scan, not once a day. "Cache hits, ~0ms" was a per-instance truth that is
  operationally false at min-instances=0.
- **With the durable GCS cache (§Approach 1, adopted):** ~14 fetches per 24h
  account-wide; the other five scans read one small GCS blob (~100–300ms). Finnhub
  free-tier limit is 60 calls/min; both shapes are well inside it.

**The NVDA-silent-exclusion lesson is honored, not waived:** a scanner skip here is
*never* silent — call leg writes a decision-record row when the gate terminates the
symbol (§DD-4), span-rejected candidates are counted in the stage-8 rejection
stats, both legs emit structured events, both events are tally-mapped for replays
(§Approach 5), and the unknown-state events are alerted (DD-6).

### DD-2 — Failure semantics: fail closed on unknown, via tri-state methods — plus the alert that bounds it

**Chosen: fail-closed, with a deployed alert on the unknown state (DD-6).** When
the calendar cannot answer for a symbol, that symbol is skipped for this scan, on
both legs — **the unknown skip is symbol-level on both legs, unchanged by the
rev 2.2 per-candidate call semantics** (a span test needs a date; no date means no
candidate can be cleared).

The options, weighed honestly:

- **Fail-closed + alert** (chosen — rev 2.1; rev 2 weighed only three options and
  under-stated the blast radius). A *transient* Finnhub outage at scan time means
  no opens account-wide for the affected scans — bounded and cheap: foregone
  7-DTE premium for part of a day, next scan retries after the 1h failure cache,
  and the durable cache (§Approach 1) means an outage only bites at all when the
  cached dates are >24h stale. But a **persistent** cause — key wiped by a
  `--set-env-vars` deploy, key revoked, Finnhub plan lapsed — blocks **all opens
  indefinitely, not for "one day"**. Nothing self-heals that; the alert is what
  bounds it. Hence the policy in DD-6 is a required deliverable of this FC, not an
  optional follow-up.
- **Fail-open-with-alert** (rejected): trades through outages ungated — the gate is
  silently absent exactly when its input is unverifiable. This codebase's signature
  disease is controls that read as live and fail open (`STATE_STORAGE_BUCKET`, the
  min-hold gate, the pre-FC-065 floor); FC-069 item 8's standing note exists
  because of it. An alert does not stop the trade.
- **Fail-closed-with-stale-cache-grace** (rejected as a *separate mechanism*, and
  rev 2's rejection rationale is corrected): rev 2 said a morning outage meets a
  cold cache "with nothing to be graceful with" — which is an argument **for
  durable storage, not against grace**. With the GCS cache adopted, the TTL itself
  *is* the grace: an entry fetched yesterday remains valid through today's outage.
  No second mechanism needed.

**Mechanism — do not flip the existing methods.** `is_earnings_within_n_days` stays
exactly as-is (the roller's gate, operator-decided unchanged; and its `None` branch
conflates "clear" with "unknown", rev 1's bug). New tri-state surface on both
calendar classes (rev 2.2: the date accessor is now primary, because the call leg
needs the *date*, not a within-N bool):

```
next_earnings_info(symbol) -> tuple[status, Optional[date]]
    ('known', date)   — earnings date within the lookahead
    ('clear', None)   — fetch succeeded, none within the lookahead (allow)
    ('unknown', None) — could not determine (fetch failed / no client / no key)
                        → gate skips the SYMBOL, both legs

earnings_within(symbol, n_days) -> Optional[bool]   # put leg; derived:
    'unknown' → None; 'clear' → False; 'known' → 0 <= (date − today) <= n_days
```

**Enabled-but-unusable degrades loudly — after the constructor fix (rev 2.1).**
As written today the no-key path does not degrade at all: it **crashes** (see
Context finding 3), and rev 2's claim that "every scan emits
`*_skipped_earnings_unknown`" described behavior the code could not produce. The
fixed contract (§Approach 1): construction with `earnings.enabled: true` and a
missing/unusable key initializes every attribute, logs one
`earnings_gate_unusable` error event, and leaves `_client = None`; thereafter
`next_earnings_info` returns `('unknown', None)` for every symbol, so every scan
skips every symbol with `*_skipped_earnings_unknown` events and
`(blocked, earnings_unknown)` rows — no exception, no `scan_failure` misfile. That
posture is FC-069 item 8's standing note: a configured-but-unresolvable dependency
must fail loudly, never silently no-op. `earnings.enabled: false` disables the
gate entirely — *disabled* and *broken* are different states with different
behavior, by design.

Fail-closed is decided, not a blocking operator question: the operator confirmed
the instinct survives (FC-069 card 4), and the blast radius — transient-bounded by
cache+retry, persistent-bounded by the alert — is restated in §Risks so sign-off
sees it.

### DD-3 — Window semantics: per-leg, decided (operator, 2026-08-03 — binding)

Informed by the step-0 exposure audit and the earnings-pop investigation
(`docs/investigations/earnings-pop-callaway-2026-08-03.md`, `e13a144`). The rev 2.1
single-knob-both-legs shape is superseded; the investigation's asymmetric-windows
analysis (§5) is the basis: the legs' exposures are mirror images but their costs
are not.

**Call leg — TRUE SPAN, per-candidate.** Block a call candidate when
`expiration_date >= next_earnings_date`, whenever earnings are known within the
lookahead. Candidates expiring **before** the event legitimately proceed.

- *Why span and not a numeric N:* any call whose expiry covers the report carries
  the full gap — AMZN C262.5 is the live demonstration ($2,308 surrendered on a
  +12.3% gap-through). A days-until N is only a proxy for span, and the operator
  **explicitly rejected hardcoding 7 or deriving N=DTE**: hardcoding manufactures
  a silent disconnect the day trading parameters extend beyond 7 DTE, and
  derive-from-DTE **over-blocks progressively at longer DTE** — a 30-DTE call
  sold a month out that *expires before the event* should be legal, and under
  N=DTE it isn't. Span is the risk predicate itself, DTE-invariant by
  construction.
- *Cost accepted, on the record:* span forfeits day-of/pre-event call premium —
  the juiciest of the cycle (~$100–250/symbol/week on this book) — **including
  the gap-down cushion**: AAPL's spanning C347.5 *profited* $165 on its −8.5%
  gap. The asymmetry is accepted because one gap-up pop costs ~$2,300 (AMZN,
  re-arm-adjusted ~$1,500–2,100) against a ~$100–200 cushion per gap-down —
  roughly 10:1 against the cushion. It also kills the adverse selection FC-073
  tracks: earnings IV is what makes the scorer *prefer* the day-of trade.
- *Lookahead invariant:* the span test is only meaningful for candidates expiring
  inside the calendar's lookahead (`earnings_lookahead_days: 90`). With call DTE
  ≤ 45 this is structurally covered; the build adds a config sanity assertion
  (`earnings_lookahead_days ≥ max call DTE + 7`) so a future DTE extension
  cannot silently outrun the calendar.

**Put leg — N=2 days-until, symbol-level.** Exactly as rev 2.1 specced: block
symbol S when `0 <= (next_earnings_date(S) − today) <= 2` in **calendar days**
(`blackout_days: 2`, now consumed by the put leg only).

- *Audit basis:* put-side realized-loss risk concentrates in **immediate
  pre-event entries** (the GOOGL 04-28 shape, `days_until = 1`), while
  wide-window pre-earnings put income was the book's **best bucket — 100% win
  rate, $301 average**. A wide put window would forfeit the best trades to block
  a risk that lives in the last two days.
- *Semantics carried from rev 2/2.1:* calendar days (trading-day semantics
  rejected — market-calendar dependency; weekend asymmetry stated and accepted);
  day-of (0) blocked; pre-earnings only — the service looks forward
  (`_from=today`), and post-earnings day-after is IV-crush territory the wheel
  should sell into, deliberately uncovered.
- *Why not span on puts too:* a 7-DTE put sold 5 days out spans the event, but
  the put leg's damage profile in ten months of fills is entry-proximity-shaped,
  not span-shaped (audit); and put-side "hold through the event" is the wheel's
  designed assignment-tolerance (FC-010). Divergent per-leg semantics are the
  deliberate choice here, not an oversight — the single-shared-knob simplicity
  argument was considered and rejected with the data on the table.

**Acceptance criterion — SATISFIED by the chosen configuration (geometry
corrected at rev 2.2).** The rev 2.1 criterion stands: the shipped gate must,
applied retroactively, block every incident on record. The audit corrected the
record: **GOOGL put `days_until = 1`; AAPL C347.5 `days_until = 0` AND spanning;
AMZN C262.5 `days_until = 0` AND spanning.** The old "AAPL Monday fill at
`days_until = 3`" premise — which drove rev 2.1's fear that N=2 fails the
flagship incident — **is falsified**; the only pre-earnings AAPL call of that
week was the day-of sale. Verification: puts-N=2 blocks the GOOGL incident
(1 ≤ 2); calls-SPAN blocks both call incidents (both expiries ≥ their event
dates). Test 7 encodes this corrected geometry, executable.

### DD-4 — Decision-record integration: two new reasons under `blocked`, schema untouched

- `REASON_EARNINGS_BLACKOUT = "earnings_blackout"` and
  `REASON_EARNINGS_UNKNOWN = "earnings_unknown"`, both added to
  `ALLOWED_REASONS[OUTCOME_BLOCKED]` in `src/data/decision_record.py`. Two reasons,
  not one: a row that says "earnings blackout" when Finnhub was down is a mislabel,
  and the closed vocabulary exists precisely so rows cannot misfile
  (`UnknownDecisionOutcome`'s reason for being).
- **Call-leg row shape under span (rev 2.2):** the `blocked{earnings_blackout}`
  row is written when the span predicate **empties the symbol's candidate set** —
  same row shape as the rev 2.1 symbol-level design, now meaning "every
  qualifying strike expired into the event". Candidates surviving the span filter
  proceed and the symbol terminates through the normal ladder. Partial span
  rejection (some candidates removed, set non-empty) is visible in the stage-8
  rejection stats (§Approach 3), not as a decision row — the row is the *symbol's*
  terminal state, and the symbol didn't terminate.
- **No new outcome** — `blocked` already means "a policy gate terminated this
  symbol at scan"; the floor reasons prove the shape.
- **No schema change.** `decision_events` columns are untouched; earnings detail
  (`next_earnings_date`, `days_until`, `earnings_hour`) rides the structured log
  event, not the record row. The `_LABEL_FIELDS` labels ride as usual. Rev 2.2
  note: the span-emptied row is written *after* basis resolution (the chain had
  to be fetched to know it emptied), so unlike the unknown-skip row it **does**
  carry `cost_basis_per_share`/`underwater` — matching the existing
  `no_candidates` row shape at that ladder position.
- Dedup key `(run_id, symbol, stage)` and the `sold`-wins ordering are unaffected.
- **Closed-enum tests:** `tests/test_decision_record.py`'s enum class gains
  `validate_outcome(OUTCOME_BLOCKED, REASON_EARNINGS_BLACKOUT/…UNKNOWN)` acceptance
  cases; the cross-pairing rejection test is extended to assert
  `validate_outcome(OUTCOME_DROPPED, REASON_EARNINGS_BLACKOUT)` still raises. No
  existing assertion weakens.
- **Cascading-impact check (docs/CLAUDE.md rule):** the dashboard reader is
  `uncovered_decisions_sql` (`dashboard/backend/services/bigquery.py:39`) —
  selects `reason` as a free string, additive-safe. The consuming card is
  `dashboard/frontend/src/components/v2/DrawdownPauseCard.tsx`, which renders
  `reason` free-form. Build step: confirm free-form rendering holds; add display
  labels for the two new reasons only if a mapping exists.

### DD-6 — Alerting: one dedicated earnings-gate alert policy (rev 2.1 — rewritten; rev 2's premise was false)

Rev 2 leaned "no new alert" on the claim that `earnings_fetch_failed` error events
"flow through the existing FC-030 error-event alerting baseline." **Verified
false** (Context finding 5): no deployed policy matches any earnings event string,
and `alert-policy.yaml` carries empty `notificationChannels`. Without a policy, a
persistent unknown-state (key wiped/revoked) blocks all opens **indefinitely and
silently** — the exact "gate reads as protective, operator learns weeks later"
failure this plan exists to end, pointed the other direction.

**Deliverable:** `deploy/monitoring/earnings_gate_alert_policy.json`, modeled
verbatim on `cost_basis_alert_policy.json` (the FC-065 P1 precedent):

- **Match filter:** `jsonPayload.event_type` ∈
  {`put_scan_skipped_earnings_unknown`, `call_scan_skipped_earnings_unknown`,
  `earnings_gate_unusable`} OR the same three as `textPayload:` substrings —
  payload matched both ways, and **no severity clause** (Cloud Run captures stderr
  as plain text with EMPTY severity; a `severity>=WARNING` clause matches nothing —
  verified by the FC-030 fire drill 2026-07-18).
- **Channel:** the existing verified email channel the cost-basis policy uses.
- **Documentation content:** what the unknown state means (Finnhub outage vs key
  wiped vs plan lapsed), triage (`gcloud logging read` on the three event types;
  check `FINNHUB_API_KEY` on the live revision; check Finnhub status), and the
  reminder that fail-closed is correct behavior, not a bug to work around — plus
  the DD-7 env lever as the emergency response.
- **Not alerted:** `*_skipped_earnings_blackout` — a blackout skip is the gate
  working; normal operation, visible in decision records and events.

### DD-7 — Kill switch: env-var override, because baked-config rollback rides a pipeline that once sat red for 11 days (rev 2.1)

`earnings.enabled` lives in `settings.yaml`, baked into the image — "atomic
config rollback" in rev 2 actually meant commit → Cloud Build → deploy, behind the
same pipeline whose silent red build delayed FC-031 by 11 days. **Adopted:**
`config.earnings_enabled` honors an env override — `EARNINGS_ENABLED` (unset →
yaml value; set → parsed boolean wins). Disabling the gate in production becomes:

```
gcloud run services update options-wheel-strategy --update-env-vars EARNINGS_ENABLED=false
```

— one command, new revision in ~1 minute, no Cloud Build, no code commit.
(**`--update-env-vars`, never `--set-env-vars`** — the latter wipes the whole env
set, the documented gotcha that is itself one of this gate's persistent-failure
scenarios.) The yaml key remains the default and the documented home; the env var
is the emergency lever, documented in the alert policy's triage text.

### DD-8 — Replay semantics of `earnings.enabled`: policy follows config, injection supplies only data (rev 2.1)

Rev 2 left this ambiguous: as specced, an injected calendar always gated, so a
live rolloff (`enabled: false`) would leave replays gated — a live/replay
divergence on exactly the FC-048 fidelity axis. **Decided:** the scanner's gate
consults `config.earnings_enabled` **regardless of injection** — an injected
calendar substitutes the *data source* (Finnhub vs point-in-time table), never the
*policy*. `earnings.enabled: false` in the active config disables the gate in
production and in replays alike; a what-if replay with the gate forced on/off is a
config override, the same way every other knob is what-if'd. Pinned by test 16b.
**Noted, not changed:** the roller seam has the pre-existing inverse quirk
(`run_rolling_cycle` checks `earnings_enabled` only when constructing its own
service; an injected calendar bypasses the check) — recorded in `docs/gates.md` as
FC-066's inheritance, untouched here.

### 1. `EarningsCalendarService` — tri-state methods, constructor fix, two-layer cache

`src/api/earnings_calendar.py`:

- **Constructor fix (rev 2.1 — required, both reviewers):** initialize **every**
  attribute (`_cache_ttl_hours`, `_lookahead_days`, `_enabled`, `_client = None`)
  *before* any early exit. The missing-key and failed-import paths no longer
  return a half-built object; they log one
  `log_error_event(error_type="earnings_gate_unusable", recoverable=True)` (when
  `earnings.enabled` is true) and leave `_client = None`. Every public method is
  callable on every construction path — the degraded service *answers*, it never
  raises.
- **Add `next_earnings_info(self, symbol) -> tuple[str, Optional[date]]`**
  (rev 2.2 — primary tri-state surface, DD-2):
  - `not self._client` → `('unknown', None)`.
  - Cached/fetched entry with `date is None` (successful fetch, nothing in the
    90d lookahead) → `('clear', None)`.
  - Entry with a date → `('known', date)`.
  - `_fetch_earnings` returned `None` (failure, incl. failure-cache window) →
    `('unknown', None)`.
- **Add `earnings_within(self, symbol, n_days) -> Optional[bool]`** — derived
  from `next_earnings_info` per DD-2's table (put leg's predicate).
- **Two-layer cache (rev 2.1 — durable layer adopted; see DD-1's topology
  numbers):**
  - **L1, module scope:** `_EARNINGS_CACHE: Dict[str, Dict]` and
    `_EARNINGS_FAILURE_CACHE: Dict[str, datetime]` at module level, plus
    `clear_earnings_cache()` for tests. Survives per-request scanner construction
    within an instance.
  - **L2, GCS blob:** one JSON object (`earnings_cache.json`, per-symbol entries
    `{date, hour, fetched_at}`) in the same bucket the opportunity store uses —
    read-through on L1 miss, write-through after any successful fetch batch.
    Entries respect the same `cache_ttl_hours` TTL. **GCS unavailability is a
    cache miss, never a block** — the ladder is L1 → L2 → Finnhub → (unknown);
    only Finnhub's answer (or its absence) determines gate state. Rationale for
    adopting: at min-instances=0 a memory-only cache is cold nearly every scan
    (≈84 calls/day, +2–6s per scan, and a morning outage meets an empty cache);
    the blob makes the 24h TTL real (~14 fetches/day) and shrinks the outage
    blast radius to "Finnhub down **and** cached dates >24h stale". Cost: one
    small GCS read per scan (~100–300ms) on an already-GCS-dependent path, and
    ~40 lines reusing the opportunity store's client/bucket plumbing.
  - **Concurrency posture, stated (reliability D12):** `/scan` runs under
    `strategy_lock`, so cache mutation is de facto serialized — the safety rests
    on that lock, recorded here so a future concurrent caller re-evaluates.
    Cheap hardening specced anyway (trader MEDIUM-7): eviction uses
    `.pop(symbol, None)`, never bare `del`, so a racing reader cannot KeyError.
- **Empty-200 ambiguity, stated and accepted with visibility (trader MEDIUM-6 /
  reliability D9):** a Finnhub 200 with an empty `earningsCalendar` is cached as
  known-clear (`{"date": None}`). Correct for ETFs (IWM/QQQ/SPY) and for genuine
  quiet windows — but a symbol-coverage regression on Finnhub's side would read
  as *permanently clear*: silent fail-open for that symbol. Accepted (the
  alternative — treating empty as unknown — bricks every ETF and quiet-window
  symbol). Visibility: the fetch's debug log gains `calendar_empty=True` on this
  path, and the audit script (§6) doubles as the retrospective detector — any
  fill inside a *table*-known window that Finnhub called clear shows up there.
  (The table is yfinance-sourced, the live service Finnhub — two independent
  providers, which is what makes the cross-check meaningful.)
- `is_earnings_within_n_days`, `get_next_earnings_date`, `get_earnings_proximity`:
  **unchanged behavior** (roller contract preserved verbatim).

### 2. `HistoricalEarningsCalendar` — same tri-state surface, plus the horizon report

`src/backtesting/engine/historical_earnings.py`. The table it serves is built by
`tools/backtesting/fetch_earnings_table.py` from **yfinance**
(`Ticker.get_earnings_dates`) — *not* Finnhub; the live service and the historical
table are deliberately independent sources. Refreshed 2026-08-03 (step 0a).

- **Add `next_earnings_info(self, symbol)`:** symbol present with an upcoming
  date → `('known', date)` (frozen-clock `_today()`); present with only past
  dates → `('clear', None)` **plus** `symbols_past_horizon.add(symbol)` (below);
  present-and-empty (ETFs) → `('clear', None)`; **absent** → `('clear', None)`
  **plus** `symbols_without_data.add(symbol)`. Add `earnings_within` derived the
  same way as the live service.
- **Horizon exhaustion becomes reportable (rev 2.1 — trader HIGH-3 / reliability
  D11):** a symbol *present* with only *past* dates otherwise answers exactly
  like known-clear — silent fail-open at the table's edge. Add
  `horizon_for(symbol) -> Optional[date]` (the symbol's last table date; `None`
  for absent/empty) and the `symbols_past_horizon` set. The simulator surfaces
  both sets in the run report: **a replay whose window end exceeds a traded
  symbol's horizon date is flagged like a data gap**, naming the symbol and its
  last covered date. ETF entries are excluded by the non-empty condition.
- **Replay fails open on table gaps, deliberately** — the one asymmetry with live,
  stated with its rationale: a replay "outage" is not a live risk, and returning
  `'unknown'` (block) for a symbol missing from the table would silently zero out
  that symbol's entire replay — a pessimistic measurement bias corrupting
  verdicts. The gap is *reported* (both sets above) so the operator refreshes the
  table instead of reading a biased verdict.
- Existing methods and the frozen-clock guard: unchanged.

## Risks & mitigations

- **Fail-closed unknown-state: transient vs persistent, stated honestly
  (rev 2.1).** *Transient* Finnhub outage: bounded — with the L2 cache, only
  symbols whose cached dates are >24h stale block; 1h failure cache retries;
  foregone 7-DTE premium for part of a day. *Persistent* cause (key wiped/revoked,
  plan lapsed): **blocks all opens indefinitely** — bounded only by the DD-6
  alert, which is why the alert policy is a required deliverable, and by the DD-7
  env lever for the operator's response.
- **Span forfeits the gap-down premium cushion (rev 2.2 — residual accepted, on
  the record):** blocking pre-event spanning calls also blocks the trades that
  *profit* when the gap goes down — AAPL's spanning C347.5 made $165 on its
  −8.5% gap; GOOGL's two July spanners made $584 combined. Accepted because the
  asymmetry is ~10:1 the other way: one gap-up pop costs ~$2,300 gross
  (~$1,500–2,100 re-arm-adjusted) against a ~$100–200 cushion per gap-down, and
  the cushion trades are exactly the IV-bait the scorer over-selects (FC-073).
- **Deploying near an earnings date with a cold cache** (rev 1's GOOGL risk, still
  real): first scan after deploy pays up to ~14 Finnhub fetches; a transient
  hiccup then blocks symbols as unknown. Mitigation: deploy outside market hours;
  the post-deploy verification scan confirms the L2 blob is populated and events
  are the expected type before the next scheduled scan.
- **`/scan` latency**: worst case (cold L1+L2, first scan after deploy) +2–6s of
  sequential fetches on the ~18.5s baseline; steady state one L2 read
  (~100–300ms) per scan and ~14 fetches/day account-wide. The per-candidate span
  test adds zero network (one date per symbol, reused). Verified in the
  post-deploy scan. (Numbers derived against the real 6-scan/min-instances=0
  topology — DD-1.)
- **A blocked symbol every scan through an earnings week reads as "scanner broke"
  to a future reader.** Mitigation: decision rows + events name the reason;
  `expires_into_earnings` appears in rejection stats; `docs/gates.md` documents
  the gate; the dashboard reason string renders free-form
  (`DrawdownPauseCard.tsx` — build-step check, DD-4).
- **Base-branch drift**: this builds on `fc-068/engine-path-deletion` pre-merge. If
  FC-068's confirmation-pass fixes touch the scanner, rebase re-verifies §3's
  ladder placement (the gate sits between named rungs — symbol names, not lines,
  are the contract).
- **Finnhub data quality** (wrong/moved dates; empty-200 coverage gaps — §1's
  accepted fail-open): unchanged risk class from FC-007; 24h TTL bounds staleness;
  `calendar_empty` field + the audit script (independent yfinance table) are the
  retrospective detectors.
- **GCS cache blob as a new dependency**: read failure falls through to Finnhub
  (test 13b); the blob can never block a trade by itself — only Finnhub's answer
  or its absence can.

1. **`tests/test_market_data.py` did not exist.** The plan's §Tests lists it as
   an existing file to extend. Created new.
2. **`find_suitable_calls` is now called with `exclude_expiry_on_or_after=None`

   five pre-existing `assert_called_once_with` assertions in
   `tests/test_options_scanner.py`. No assertion was weakened; the kwarg was
   added to each.
3. **`_build_client` added as a patchable seam on `EarningsCalendarService`**
   (not in the plan). Without it, whether a test sees a working gate depends on

   sentinel; the no-key path returns before it, so test 10 is unaffected.

   today (`get_option_chain_with_analysis` drops contracts with no expiry),
   which is precisely why it must not be the one path that fails open. Pinned by
   `test_an_unparseable_expiry_is_rejected_not_waved_through`.
5. **The `expires_into_earnings` counter is incremented LAST in the criteria
   chain**, after delta/DTE/premium/liquidity and the cost-basis floor. The plan
   says "in the per-contract criteria chain" without fixing the position. Last
   is what makes §3's emptied-set predicate correct: the counter then means "a

   unaffected.
7. **`EARNINGS_ENABLED` ignores an unparseable value** (yaml wins, with a
   warning) rather than treating it as false. DD-7 does not specify the invalid
   case; a typo silently disabling a risk control is the failure this whole plan
   is about.
8. **Horizon/data-gap reporting reaches the operator through
   `SimulationResult` → `evaluate._data_quality`** as
   `earnings_symbols_missing_from_table` / `earnings_symbols_past_table_horizon`.

   the carrier; the data-quality block is where the report's other coverage
   facts already live.
9. **The runbook section landed in `deploy/monitoring/drawdown_pause_alert.md`

   cost-basis policy already points at it.
10. **L2 is written per successful fetch, not per batch** (§Approach 1 says
    "write-through after any successful fetch batch"). Shipped per-fetch because
    it is durable against a scan that dies partway: a batch write loses every
    date fetched before the crash, and the next cold scan refetches all of them.
    Cost is bounded and latency-only — worst case ~14 small serialised writes on
    a fully cold scan (~50–100ms each), on a path already inside
    `strategy_lock`, and every write failure is swallowed as a no-op with the
    entry still in L1. Steady state is one write per changed symbol per 24h.
11. **An unparseable expiry is counted under `expires_into_earnings`** rather

    the FC-068 §5 anti-pattern in miniature, so the debug log carries
    `expiration_date=None` to disambiguate instead. Revisit if the counter ever
    fires with a null expiry in production.
